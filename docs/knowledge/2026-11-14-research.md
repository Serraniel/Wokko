# Kitchen knowledge: research for the built-in pack

Prepared 2026-11-14 as source material for the kitchen knowledge pack (`backend/internal/knowledge/data/`, plan in `docs/plans/2026-11-14-kitchen-knowledge.md`). Entries in the pack name these sources.
All text is in my own words. Every item names its source. Markers:
- **[uncertain]**: sources disagree, or I couldn't confirm it against a primary source.
- **[brand]**: depends on the product or recipe, so a rule should say "may contain".
- **[heuristic]**: a rule that gives a hint, not a fact. It should be shown as info, not as a warning.

## 1. Allergens (EU 14)

Legal basis: **EU-FIC** Annex II. Its exemptions matter for rules. Where the regulation exempts a product, Wokko should still say "may contain" rather than "free of", because a household recipe can't prove how refined an ingredient is.

General notes for the engine:
- Annex II names the **nuts** exactly. Coconut, pine nuts, nutmeg, chestnut, water chestnut and tiger nut are **not** EU-14 nuts. Pine nuts are a known allergen in some people but aren't declarable.
- **Buckwheat** (Buchweizen), quinoa, amaranth, millet (Hirse), rice, corn and teff contain no gluten. Cross-contact is common **[brand]**.
- Plain **ghee** and **butter** count as milk. They are mostly fat but carry milk protein.

### 1.1 Cereals containing gluten · Glutenhaltiges Getreide
Annex II: wheat (incl. spelt and khorasan), rye, barley, oats and their hybrids. Exempt: wheat-based glucose syrups incl. dextrose, wheat maltodextrins, barley glucose syrups, and cereals used for distillates such as spirits. (**EU-FIC**)
- Grains / Getreide: wheat (Weizen), spelt (Dinkel), green spelt (Grünkern), emmer, einkorn, khorasan/Kamut, durum, rye (Roggen), barley (Gerste), triticale, oats (Hafer).
- Products: flour (Mehl), semolina (Grieß, Hartweizengrieß), couscous, bulgur, freekeh, pasta and noodles (Nudeln), bread (Brot), breadcrumbs and panko (Paniermehl, Semmelbrösel), seitan (pure wheat gluten), Spätzle, dumplings (Knödel), puff and shortcrust pastry (Blätterteig, Mürbeteig), croutons.
- Non-obvious:
  - Soy sauce (Sojasauce): usually brewed with wheat. Tamari is often wheat-free but not always **[brand]**.
  - Beer (Bier) and malt (Malz), malt extract, malt vinegar (Malzessig).
  - Worcestershire sauce: the UK original uses malt vinegar (barley). Some markets use spirit vinegar **[brand]**. (**FSA-14** lists it among sources)
  - Hoisin, oyster sauce, teriyaki, some miso (barley miso, mugi), some curry and spice blends, stock cubes and gravy granules (Brühwürfel, Bratensoßenbinder) **[brand]**.
  - Roux (Mehlschwitze), beurre manié, gravy, dredged meat ("in Mehl wenden"), batter (Backteig).
  - Some sausages and meat products (Würstchen, Leberkäse) **[brand]**.
  - Asafoetida (Asant) powder is usually cut with wheat flour **[brand]**.
- Depends:
  - **Oats** are listed in Annex II, but pure oats can be sold as "gluten-free" if gluten is ≤ 20 mg/kg (**EU-GF**). Most coeliacs tolerate such oats; some react to avenin. Rule: plain oats → "contains gluten (unless labelled gluten-free)".
  - **Glucose syrup** and **dextrose** from wheat are exempt (**EU-FIC**). Don't flag them.
  - Spirits (whisky, vodka from wheat) are exempt as distillates (**EU-FIC**). Beer is not.

### 1.2 Crustaceans · Krebstiere
- Prawns and shrimp (Garnelen, Shrimps, Krabben, Nordseekrabben), crab (Krebs, Taschenkrebs), lobster (Hummer), langoustine/scampi (Kaisergranat, Scampi), crayfish (Flusskrebs), krill.
- Non-obvious:
  - Shrimp paste (Garnelenpaste; belacan, kapi, terasi), in many **Thai curry pastes** (Currypaste) and sambals **[brand]**.
  - Dried shrimp (getrocknete Garnelen), XO sauce, some fish sauces and kimchi (salted shrimp, saeujeot) **[brand]**.
  - Bisque, Nantua sauce (crayfish butter), cocktail/Marie Rose with prawns, surimi (sometimes crab extract) **[brand]**.
  - Glucosamine is not a food ingredient; ignore.

### 1.3 Eggs · Eier
- Whole egg, yolk, white (Ei, Eigelb, Eiweiß, Eiklar), egg powder, albumen, lysozyme (E1105).
- Non-obvious:
  - Mayonnaise, aioli (modern versions), remoulade, tartare sauce, hollandaise, béarnaise, Caesar dressing, sauce gribiche.
  - Fresh pasta and egg noodles (Eiernudeln, Spätzle), meringue (Baiser), marshmallow (some), nougat, macarons, custard (Vanillesoße, Pudding when made from scratch), zabaglione, tiramisu.
  - Egg wash (mit Ei bestreichen), breading (panieren: flour, egg, crumbs), binding in meatballs and burgers (Frikadellen, Buletten), quiche, soufflé, Kaiserschmarrn.
  - Some cheeses use **lysozyme from egg** as a preservative, notably **Grana Padano** **[uncertain]**: I couldn't confirm this against the consortium's specification. Treat as "check label".
  - Wine fined with egg white must declare egg if detectable (**FSAI-WINE**).

### 1.4 Fish · Fisch
Exempt: fish gelatine used as a carrier for vitamins or carotenoids, and fish gelatine or **isinglass** used to fine beer and wine (**EU-FIC**).
- Any fish, roe (Rogen, Kaviar), fish stock (Fischfond, fumet), dashi made with bonito (Katsuobushi).
- Non-obvious:
  - **Worcestershire sauce**: anchovies.
  - **Fish sauce** (Fischsauce; nam pla, nước mắm), many Thai/Vietnamese dressings (nuoc cham), some kimchi **[brand]**.
  - **Caesar dressing**, **tapenade** (often), salsa verde (Italian, often), puttanesca, vitello tonnato (tuna), bagna cauda, pissaladière.
  - Surimi and crab sticks (Surimi, Krebsfleischimitat).
  - Some Thai curry pastes, oyster sauce rarely **[brand]**.
  - Omega-3 fortified products (fish oil) **[brand]**.

### 1.5 Peanuts · Erdnüsse
- Peanuts, peanut butter (Erdnussbutter, Erdnussmus), groundnut oil (Erdnussöl), peanut flour.
- Non-obvious: satay sauce, gado-gado, some Thai curries (massaman), kung pao, African peanut stew (maafe), Erdnussflips, some vegan cheeses and pestos, some curry pastes and chili crisps **[brand]**.
- Note: unlike soy oil, **refined peanut oil is not exempted** in Annex II. Flag it.

### 1.6 Soybeans · Sojabohnen (Soja)
Exempt: fully refined soybean oil and fat, natural tocopherols (E306) and tocopherol forms from soy, phytosterols and plant stanol esters from soy oil (**EU-FIC**).
- Soybeans, edamame, tofu, tempeh, soy milk (Sojadrink), soy flour, textured soy protein (TVP, Sojaschnetzel), yuba.
- Non-obvious: **soy sauce**, tamari, **miso**, teriyaki, hoisin, black bean sauce, doubanjiang (fermented broad bean, usually with soy), **soy lecithin** (E322) in chocolate and baking, many vegan meat substitutes, stock cubes **[brand]**.
- Depends: "vegetable oil" may be soy oil, which is exempt if fully refined.

### 1.7 Milk · Milch (einschließlich Laktose)
Exempt: whey used for distillates, and lactitol (**EU-FIC**).
- Milk, cream (Sahne, Schlagsahne, Schmand, saure Sahne, Crème fraîche), butter, buttermilk, ghee/butterschmalz, yogurt (Joghurt), quark, cheese (Käse), mascarpone, ricotta, kefir, whey (Molke), casein, lactose, milk powder, condensed milk (Kondensmilch).
- Non-obvious:
  - Pesto alla genovese (Parmigiano/Pecorino).
  - Béchamel, Mornay, cream sauces, gratins, mashed potatoes (Kartoffelpüree), naan, brioche, milk bread, croissants.
  - Milk chocolate, white chocolate, many dark chocolates (traces or milk fat) **[brand]**.
  - Some sausages and ham (lactose, milk protein), some stock cubes, "lactose-free" milk still contains milk protein.
  - Wine fined with casein must declare milk if detectable (**FSAI-WINE**).
- Not milk: coconut milk, oat/almond "milk" (but almond = nuts, oat = gluten), cocoa butter, peanut butter.

### 1.8 Nuts · Schalenfrüchte
Annex II names: almonds (Mandeln), hazelnuts (Haselnüsse), walnuts (Walnüsse), cashews (Cashewnüsse), pecans (Pekannüsse), Brazil nuts (Paranüsse), pistachios (Pistazien), macadamia/Queensland nuts (Macadamianüsse). Exempt: nuts used for distillates (**EU-FIC**).
- Non-obvious:
  - **Marzipan** (almonds), frangipane, amaretti, Bethmännchen, Florentiner, Makronen. Persipan uses apricot or peach kernels, which are not EU-14 nuts, but cross-contact is common **[brand]**.
  - Praline, gianduja, nougat (often hazelnut/almond), hazelnut spread (Nuss-Nougat-Creme).
  - Pesto variants with cashew or walnut, pesto alla trapanese (almonds), romesco (almonds/hazelnuts), muhammara (walnuts), dukkah (hazelnuts), korma (cashews/almonds), baklava (walnuts/pistachios).
  - Nut oils (walnut, hazelnut oil) are not exempt.
  - Almond milk, almond flour (Mandelmehl, gemahlene Mandeln).
- Depends: amaretto, Frangelico and other liqueurs are distillate-based, so often exempt **[uncertain/brand]**: the exemption covers the distillate, not nuts added for flavour later.

### 1.9 Celery · Sellerie
- Celery stalks (Staudensellerie, Stangensellerie), celeriac (Knollensellerie), celery leaves, celery seed (Selleriesamen), **celery salt** (Selleriesalz), celery powder.
- Non-obvious (**FSA-14** names salads, meat products, soups and stock cubes):
  - **Stock cubes and instant broth** (Brühwürfel, Gemüsebrühe, Brühpulver) **[brand]**, very common.
  - **Suppengrün** (bundle of carrot, leek, celeriac, parsley).
  - Mirepoix and soffritto (onion, carrot, celery), Bolognese, many soups and stews, Waldorf salad.
  - **Bloody Mary** (celery salt).
  - Spice blends: curry powder, some seasoning salts and Würzmischungen **[brand]**.
  - Cured meats labelled "nitrite-free" or "organic" often use **celery powder** as a natural nitrite source **[brand]**.

### 1.10 Mustard · Senf
- Mustard (Senf: mittelscharf, Dijon, süßer Senf), mustard seeds (Senfkörner, Senfsaat), mustard powder, mustard greens and leaves, mustard oil.
- Non-obvious: mayonnaise and vinaigrette (often), remoulade, honey-mustard dressing, curry powders and Indian tempering (black mustard seed), piccalilli, pickles (Gewürzgurken often use mustard seed), some ketchups, sausages, Leberkäse, Königsberger Klopse (some recipes), sauce Robert, devilled eggs **[brand]**.

### 1.11 Sesame seeds · Sesamsamen
- Sesame seeds (Sesam), tahini (Sesammus, Tahin), sesame oil (Sesamöl).
- Non-obvious: **hummus**, baba ghanoush, halva, **za'atar** (usually), furikake, gomasio, dukkah (often), burger buns and bagels, sesame snaps (Sesamkrokant), some sushi, gomae dressing, some Chinese sauces.
- Note: sesame oil is **not** exempt, even refined.

### 1.12 Sulphur dioxide and sulphites · Schwefeldioxid und Sulphite
Declarable above **10 mg/kg or 10 mg/l**, as total SO₂ in the product as eaten (**EU-FIC**). A recipe can't measure that, so Wokko should say "may contain".
- Wine (Wein), sparkling wine, port, sherry, vermouth, cider, some beers.
- **Wine vinegar** (Weinessig, Balsamico), some bottled lemon juice.
- **Dried fruit**: apricots (Aprikosen, getrocknet), raisins and sultanas (sometimes), figs, dried mango (unsulphured exist) **[brand]**.
- Some sausages, frozen prawns, pickles, potato products (dried, frozen), dried mushrooms, gelatine (sometimes), glacé cherries **[brand]**.
- Also named as E220 to E228.

### 1.13 Lupin · Lupinen
- Lupin flour (Lupinenmehl), lupin seeds/beans (Lupinenkerne, lupini), lupin protein.
- Non-obvious: **FSA-14** notes lupin flour in some breads, pastries and pasta; common in French, Belgian and Italian baked goods. Increasingly in **vegan and gluten-free products** (Lupinen-Tofu, Lupinenkaffee, meat substitutes) **[brand]**.
- Cross-reactivity with peanut is reported; that's for doctors, not Wokko.

### 1.14 Molluscs · Weichtiere
- Mussels (Miesmuscheln), clams (Venusmuscheln, Vongole), oysters (Austern), scallops (Jakobsmuscheln), squid (Tintenfisch, Kalmar, Calamari), octopus (Krake, Pulpo), cuttlefish (Sepia), snails (Schnecken, escargots), whelks, abalone.
- Non-obvious: **oyster sauce** (Austernsauce), squid ink (Sepiatinte) in pasta and risotto nero, XO sauce (dried scallop), paella and seafood mixes (Meeresfrüchte), some fish sauces **[brand]**.

## 3. Sauce families

Detection uses food classes plus a technique from step text. "Roux" means fat + wheat flour cooked together before the liquid (`Mehlschwitze`, `anschwitzen` with flour, `roux`). Sources: **ESC** for the classic system, **CIA** for proportions, **McGEE** for the science.

Note: Escoffier's own *grandes sauces* were espagnole, velouté, béchamel and tomato, with hollandaise and mayonnaise as basic sauces of their own. The "five mother sauces" list (with hollandaise) is a later, mainly American-taught convention (**CIA**). Both are common; Wokko can use the five.

### 3.1 French mother sauces

**Béchamel · Béchamel (Béchamelsoße)**
- Detect: `fat.butter` + `starch.wheat_flour` as roux, then `liquid.milk`. Often nutmeg, onion (clouté), bay.
- Derivatives:
  - **Mornay**: + grated cheese (Gruyère, Parmesan), sometimes yolk. DE: Mornaysoße, Käsesoße.
  - **Soubise**: + onion purée (sweated, not browned).
  - **Crème / cream sauce**: + cream. DE: Rahmsoße (loosely).
  - **Nantua**: + crayfish butter and cream (crustacean).
  - **Cheddar / cheese sauce** (mac and cheese): + cheddar, often mustard.

**Velouté · Velouté**
- Detect: roux (white or blond) + light stock (`liquid.stock.poultry`, `.veal`, `.fish`, `.vegetable`). No milk.
- Derivatives:
  - **Suprême**: chicken velouté + cream, reduced. DE: Geflügelrahmsoße.
  - **Allemande** (also *sauce parisienne*): veal or chicken velouté + egg-yolk-and-cream liaison, often lemon.
  - **Poulette**: allemande + mushroom, lemon, parsley.
  - **Aurore**: velouté + tomato purée.
  - **Sauce vin blanc / white wine sauce**: fish velouté + white wine, fish fumet, butter. **Bercy**: + shallot, white wine, parsley. **Normande**: fish velouté + mushroom liquor, cream, yolk.
  - **Ivoire**: suprême + meat glaze.
  - German classics on a velouté base: **Frikassee**, **Königsberger Klopse** (+ capers), **Ragout fin**, Hühnerfrikassee, helle Soße / Senfsoße (+ mustard), Meerrettichsoße (+ horseradish), Dillsoße.

**Espagnole (brown sauce) · Espagnole, braune Grundsoße**
- Detect: browned roux (or browned bones) + brown stock (`liquid.stock.brown`, `.beef`, `.veal` with roasting cues) + mirepoix + tomato paste. Cues: `rösten`, `Tomatenmark anrösten`, `brown`, `dunkel`.
- **Demi-glace**: espagnole + brown stock reduced by about half.
- Derivatives (usually from demi-glace):
  - **Bordelaise**: red wine, shallot, thyme, bone marrow.
  - **Chasseur / hunter's sauce**: mushrooms, shallot, white wine, tomato, herbs. DE: Jägersoße (modern versions often skip the espagnole).
  - **Robert**: onion, white wine, mustard.
  - **Lyonnaise**: onion, vinegar.
  - **Madeira / Madère**, **Port** (Portweinsoße): reduced fortified wine.
  - **Poivrade**: pepper, vinegar, often for game. **Diable**: white wine, shallot, cayenne.
  - **Bigarade**: bitter orange, for duck.
  - German **Bratensoße** and **Rahmsoße für Wild** are usually built on this logic (brown stock, roasted vegetables, wine, often thickened with starch instead of roux).

**Hollandaise · Hollandaise (Sauce hollandaise)**
- Detect: `egg.yolk` + `fat.butter` (melted or clarified) + acid (`acid.lemon` or vinegar reduction), warm (`Wasserbad`, `bain-marie`, `aufschlagen`, `whisk over simmering water`).
- Derivatives:
  - **Béarnaise**: reduction of vinegar/white wine, shallot, tarragon (Estragon), chervil (Kerbel), pepper.
  - **Choron**: béarnaise + tomato paste/purée.
  - **Foyot / Valois**: béarnaise + meat glaze.
  - **Paloise**: béarnaise with mint instead of tarragon.
  - **Maltaise**: hollandaise + blood orange juice and zest.
  - **Mousseline / Chantilly**: hollandaise + whipped cream.

**Tomato sauce · Tomatensoße (Sauce tomate)**
- Detect: tomatoes (fresh, canned, passata) cooked down (`einkochen`, `köcheln`, `reduzieren`, `simmer`), with aromatics. Escoffier's version used salt pork, mirepoix and a little roux; modern versions don't.
- Derivatives: **Portugaise** (+ onion, garlic, tomato concassé), **Provençale** (+ garlic, olive oil, herbs), **Créole** (+ pepper, onion, celery). Italian relatives (not French derivatives): marinara/napoletana, arrabbiata (+ chili), puttanesca (+ olives, capers, anchovy), amatriciana (+ guanciale, pecorino), ragù alla bolognese (meat sauce, technically a ragù).

### 3.2 Other families

**Cold emulsions**
- **Mayonnaise · Mayonnaise**: `egg.yolk` + `oil` added slowly while whisking + acid (lemon or vinegar), often mustard (lecithin and mustard mucilage help emulsify). Derivatives: **aioli** (garlic; traditional Provençal aioli is garlic + oil only), **remoulade** (pickles, capers, herbs, anchovy), **tartare** (pickles, capers, herbs, often chopped egg), **rouille** (saffron, chili, bread or potato), **cocktail / Marie Rose** (+ ketchup), **andalouse** (+ tomato, pepper). **Gribiche** uses cooked yolks. (**ESC**, **McGEE**)
- **Vinaigrette · Vinaigrette**: oil + acid (vinegar, lemon), classic ratio about **3 parts oil : 1 part acid** (**CIA**), often mustard as emulsifier; a temporary emulsion that separates on standing. DE: Essig-Öl-Dressing, Salatsoße.

**Warm butter emulsions**
- **Beurre blanc · Beurre blanc**: reduction of white wine + vinegar + shallot, then cold butter whisked in piece by piece off a strong heat. Must be kept warm, not hot: it splits when it gets too hot and the butter sets when it cools **[uncertain]**: published holding ranges vary; roughly 50 to 60 °C is common advice. **Beurre rouge**: red wine. **Beurre nantais**: often with a little cream (cream stabilises).
- **Monter au beurre / finishing with butter · Montieren (mit kalter Butter)**: cold butter swirled into a pan sauce at the end, off the boil.

**Pan sauce, jus, gravy**
- **Pan sauce · Pfannensoße**: sear meat, pour off fat, deglaze the fond (Bratensatz) with wine or stock, reduce, finish with butter or cream. Detect: sear step + `ablöschen`/`deglaze` in the same pan + reduce.
- **Jus · Jus, Bratensaft**: roasting juices and stock reduced, unthickened. **Jus lié**: lightly thickened with starch (cornstarch, arrowroot).
- **Gravy · Bratensoße, Soße**: pan drippings + flour (roux in the pan) + stock, simmered. British/US style.
- **Glace** (meat glaze, Fleischglace): stock reduced to a syrup that sets when cold.

**Pesto**
- **Pesto alla genovese · Pesto Genovese**: basil, pine nuts, garlic, coarse salt, Parmigiano and/or Pecorino (Fiore Sardo), extra-virgin olive oil, traditionally pounded in a mortar. Not cooked; added off the heat (heat darkens basil). Allergens: milk; pine nuts are not EU-14 nuts.
- Related: **pesto rosso** (sun-dried tomatoes, often almonds), **pesto alla trapanese** (tomato, almonds), **pistou** (Provençal, no nuts), **Bärlauchpesto** (wild garlic).

**Main Asian bases**
- **Dashi · Dashi**: kombu + katsuobushi (bonito) steeped, not boiled hard. Vegan: kombu + dried shiitake.
- **Teriyaki · Teriyaki**: soy sauce + mirin + sake + sugar, reduced to a glaze.
- **Tare (Japan)**: concentrated seasoning base for ramen/yakitori (soy, mirin, sake, sometimes stock).
- **Ponzu · Ponzu**: citrus juice + soy sauce + dashi + mirin/vinegar.
- **Chinese brown sauce / stir-fry sauce**: stock + soy + oyster sauce + Shaoxing wine + sugar, thickened with a cornstarch slurry at the end.
- **Sweet and sour · Süß-sauer-Soße**: vinegar + sugar + ketchup or fruit, cornstarch slurry.
- **Hoisin · Hoisin**: fermented soy paste, sugar, garlic, spices.
- **Black bean sauce**: fermented black soybeans (douchi), garlic, ginger.
- **Thai curry paste · Thai-Currypaste**: chilies, lemongrass, galangal, garlic, shallot, coriander root, kaffir lime, usually shrimp paste; fried in coconut cream ("crack" the cream until the oil separates).
- **Nước chấm**: fish sauce + lime + sugar + water + garlic + chili.
- **Sambal (Indonesian/Malaysian)**: ground chili base, often with shrimp paste.
- **Gochujang-based**: fermented chili-soy-rice paste (gochujang often contains wheat **[brand]**).
- **Satay / peanut sauce**: peanuts, coconut milk, soy, chili.
- **Indian masala base**: onion browned, then ginger-garlic paste, ground spices, tomato, cooked until oil separates (`bhuna`). **Tadka / tarka**: whole spices bloomed in hot ghee or oil, poured over at the end.

## 4. Thickening and binding

### 4.1 Roux · Mehlschwitze
- **Ratio:** equal weights fat and flour (**CIA**, **ESC**). Butter is about 80 % fat, so some cooks use slightly more butter.
- **Colors** (**CIA**):
  - White (weiß/hell): cooked a few minutes, no color. For béchamel and light veloutés.
  - Blond (blond): straw color, nutty smell. For veloutés.
  - Brown (braun): longer cooking, nutty, for espagnole.
  - Dark (dunkel, Cajun "chocolate roux"): long cooking, often in oil.
  - The darker the roux, the **less it thickens**, because heat breaks starch into smaller pieces (dextrinization). A dark roux needs more roux for the same thickness (**McGEE**).
- **Amount per liter of liquid** (**CIA**, converted from oz per gallon, so approximate):
  - Light (soup): about 75 to 90 g roux per liter.
  - Medium (sauce): about 90 to 105 g per liter (classic béchamel ≈ 50 g butter + 50 g flour per liter).
  - Heavy (binding, croquettes): about 120 to 135 g per liter.
- **Technique:** contrasting temperatures (hot roux + cold/room-temperature liquid, or cold roux + hot liquid), added gradually, whisking (**CIA**). Bring to a boil, then simmer (CIA: at least about 20 to 30 min for a classic sauce to lose the raw flour taste and reach full gloss; home recipes often use less).
- **Failure modes:**
  - Lumps: hot roux + hot liquid all at once; liquid added too fast; not whisked. Fix: whisk hard, or strain through a sieve, or blend.
  - Raw floury taste, dull look: not simmered long enough.
  - Gluey/pasty: thickened, then reduced without first reaching a boil (from the plan's first batch), or too much roux.
  - Burnt roux (black specks, bitter): heat too high; must be discarded.
  - Thin sauce with dark roux: dextrinization; use more roux or add a little blond roux.
  - Greasy surface: too much fat relative to flour.

### 4.2 Starch slurries: cornstarch and potato starch · Speisestärke (Maisstärke), Kartoffelstärke
- **Method:** mix the starch with cold liquid first (about 1 part starch to 2 parts cold water), then stir into the simmering liquid. Dry starch into hot liquid lumps.
- **Amount:** roughly 1 tablespoon (≈ 8 to 10 g) cornstarch thickens about 250 ml to a medium sauce; about 30 to 40 g per liter **[heuristic]**. Cornstarch thickens about twice as much as flour by weight (**ATK**).
- **Temperatures** (**McGEE**): starches swell over a range. Cornstarch needs to come close to a boil (about 90 to 95 °C) to thicken fully and lose its starchy cloudiness. Potato starch thickens at lower temperatures (around 60 to 70 °C **[uncertain: ranges vary by source]**), so it is often stirred in off the boil.
- **Long boiling (key fact):** a starch-thickened liquid is thickest right around the boil. **Keeping it boiling for long, stirring hard, or heating it again and again breaks the swollen granules, and the sauce thins** (**McGEE**). Potato starch is more sensitive than cornstarch. One reference puts the tolerance at only a few minutes of boiling **[uncertain: exact minutes vary]**. Practical rule: add the slurry at the end, boil briefly (≈ 1 min), then stop.
- **Other failure modes:**
  - **Acid** (lots of vinegar, citrus, fruit) weakens starch over long cooking; add acid after thickening or use a bit more starch (**McGEE**).
  - **Sugar** in large amounts delays and weakens gelation (fruit fillings).
  - **Egg yolk amylase:** raw yolks contain an enzyme that breaks starch. A starch-and-yolk custard or pie filling that isn't brought to a full boil after the yolks go in can turn runny overnight (**McGEE**).
  - **Freezing:** cornstarch and flour sauces weep water after thawing (syneresis); waxy starches and tapioca hold up better.
  - **Cooling:** cornstarch sets into a gel when cold; potato starch sauces stay more fluid.
  - **Arrowroot** (Pfeilwurzelmehl) stays clear and tolerates acid, but turns slimy with dairy **[uncertain: common advice, not verified against a primary source]**.
  - **Saliva** amylase from a tasting spoon can thin a pudding **[uncertain, anecdotal]**. Not worth a rule.

### 4.3 Beurre manié · Mehlbutter
- Equal weights soft butter and flour kneaded to a paste (**CIA**, **ESC**).
- Whisk small pieces into a simmering liquid at the end; simmer a few minutes so the flour loses its raw taste.
- Good for adjusting a sauce at the end (stews, coq au vin). Too much gives a pasty texture and taste.

### 4.4 Liaison (egg yolk and cream) · Legierung (Eigelb und Sahne)
- **Ratio:** about **1 part yolk to 3 parts cream** by volume (**CIA**). Roughly 1 yolk + 50 to 60 ml cream per 250 to 500 ml of sauce, depending on richness **[heuristic]**.
- **Tempering:** whisk some hot sauce into the yolk-cream mix first (Temperieren), then stir the mix back into the sauce, off the boil.
- **Heat:** thickens through yolk protein setting. Yolks start to thicken around 65 °C and curdle somewhere around 70 to 85 °C (**McGEE** gives about 70 to 77 °C for egg sauces; other sources say full curdling around 85 °C) **[uncertain: exact figure varies]**. Rule: keep it below a simmer once the yolks are in.
- **Starch protects yolks:** a sauce already thickened with flour or starch can be heated much hotter, even briefly boiled, without curdling, because starch slows protein coagulation (**McGEE**). That's why the frikassee example gets only a note.
- **Failure modes:** curdled, grainy sauce from boiling without starch, or from adding yolks straight into a boiling liquid; scrambled egg bits when not tempered.

### 4.5 Reduction · Reduktion, Einkochen
- Evaporating water concentrates flavour, gelatine, sugar and **salt**. Season at the end.
- Wine needs reducing to soften raw alcohol taste and acidity; it doesn't remove all alcohol **[uncertain on exact residual %]**.
- Failure modes: over-salted (salted before reducing, salty stock or stock cubes); bitter or burnt (reduced too far, scorched at the edge); stock reduced from too little gelatine stays thin; acidic or tannic wine turns harsh when reduced hard.

### 4.6 Gelatine · Gelatine
- Forms: sheets (Blattgelatine) and powder (gemahlene Gelatine). German retail: **6 sheets ≈ 1 sachet ground gelatine (≈ 9 g)**, enough to set **about 500 ml** of liquid **[uncertain: check packaging; sheet weight and bloom strength differ by brand and country]**.
- **Method:** soak sheets in cold water 5 to 10 min, squeeze, dissolve in warm (not boiling) liquid. Powder: sprinkle on cold liquid to bloom, then warm. Stir dissolved gelatine into cold mixtures carefully: first mix a little of the cold mixture into the warm gelatine (Angleichen), or it sets in strings.
- **Do not boil**: prolonged high heat weakens the gel.
- **Set and melt:** sets in the fridge over several hours; melts at about body temperature (around 30 to 35 °C), which is why it melts in the mouth (**FAO-AGAR** comparison).
- **Enzymes:** raw **pineapple, kiwi, papaya, fresh fig, mango** (also ginger root, passion fruit **[uncertain]**) contain proteases that digest gelatine, so it doesn't set. Cooked, canned or pasteurized fruit is fine.
- Very acidic or alcoholic mixtures need more gelatine.
- Failure modes: rubbery (too much), soft or weeping (too little, boiled, enzymes), lumps or strings (cold mixture added too fast), separated layers (gelatine added too hot to cream).

### 4.7 Agar-agar · Agar-Agar
- Seaweed-derived, vegan.
- **Must be boiled** (about 1 to 2 min) to dissolve; it doesn't dissolve in warm liquid.
- A 1 % solution sets at about **32 to 39 °C** and stays firm until about **85 °C** (**FAO-AGAR**). So it sets at room temperature and survives warm serving.
- **Amount:** around 0.8 to 1 g per 100 ml for a firm gel; more for acidic liquids (one source: 0.9 g/100 ml neutral, 1.3 g/100 ml acidic) **[uncertain: brands vary widely in strength; test first]**.
- Unaffected by fruit proteases. Strong acid (pH below about 4) weakens it: dissolve agar first, add acid after.
- Texture: firmer and more brittle than gelatine; doesn't "melt in the mouth".
- Failure modes: doesn't set (not boiled, too acidic, too little); too hard and brittle (too much); weeping liquid (syneresis) on standing.

## 5. Core techniques

Trigger words should match on normalized text (lowercase, umlauts folded). Some German verbs are ambiguous; ambiguity noted. Sources: **CIA**, **McGEE**.

### 5.1 Sweat · Anschwitzen, glasig dünsten
- Triggers DE: `anschwitzen`, `glasig dünsten`, `glasig anschwitzen`, `farblos anschwitzen`, `dünsten` (also means steaming in little liquid). EN: `sweat`, `soften without colour`, `until translucent`, `until soft but not browned`.
- Tips:
  - Low to medium heat, fat, often a lid: you want the vegetables soft and sweet, with no browning, which keeps light sauces and stocks pale.
  - A pinch of salt early draws out moisture and helps them soften without colouring.

### 5.2 Sauté · Sautieren, Schwenken
- Triggers DE: `sautieren`, `schwenken`, `in der Pfanne anbraten` (ambiguous with sear), `kurzbraten`. EN: `sauté`, `pan-fry`, `stir-fry` (wok: `im Wok braten`, `pfannenrühren`).
- Tips:
  - Hot pan before the food goes in, and don't crowd it: crowding drops the temperature, water can't evaporate, and the food steams grey instead of browning.
  - Cut pieces to similar size so they cook evenly; dry wet vegetables first.

### 5.3 Sear · Scharf anbraten
- Triggers DE: `scharf anbraten`, `rundherum anbraten`, `von allen Seiten anbraten`, `anbraten` (generic), `bräunen`. EN: `sear`, `brown on all sides`, `brown well`.
- Tips:
  - Pat the meat dry and let the pan get very hot: surface water must evaporate before browning (above 100 °C) can start.
  - Leave it until it releases from the pan before turning. Searing builds flavour; it does **not** "seal in juices" (**McGEE**).

### 5.4 Braise · Schmoren
- Triggers DE: `schmoren`, `Schmorgericht`, `Schmortopf`, `im Bräter`, `zugedeckt im Ofen garen`, `Gulasch`, `Ragout`, `Rouladen`. EN: `braise`, `stew`, `pot roast`, `cover and cook in the oven`.
- Tips:
  - Brown first, then add liquid to come partway up, cover, and cook gently (low oven around 150 to 160 °C, or a bare simmer). Collagen in tough cuts turns into gelatine only over long, moist, gentle heat (**McGEE**).
  - Use collagen-rich cuts (shoulder, shin, cheek). Lean cuts like chicken breast or pork tenderloin dry out in a long braise.

### 5.5 Poach · Pochieren, Garziehen
- Triggers DE: `pochieren`, `gar ziehen lassen`, `ziehen lassen` (also used for steeping tea and marinating), `simmern` (rare), `unter dem Siedepunkt`. EN: `poach`, `barely simmering`, `just below a simmer`.
- Tips:
  - Keep the liquid below a simmer, roughly 70 to 85 °C, with the surface barely moving (**CIA**). Boiling toughens and dries delicate proteins (fish, chicken breast, eggs).
  - For poached eggs, very fresh eggs keep their shape; a splash of vinegar helps the white set faster.

### 5.6 Blanch and shock · Blanchieren und Abschrecken
- Triggers DE: `blanchieren`, `kurz überbrühen`, `in kochendem Salzwasser kurz garen`, `abschrecken`, `in Eiswasser`, `eiskalt abschrecken`. EN: `blanch`, `shock`, `refresh`, `plunge into ice water`, `ice bath`.
- Tips:
  - Use plenty of boiling water so the temperature doesn't drop when the vegetables go in; short cooking keeps them bright green and crisp (**McGEE**).
  - Ice water stops the cooking at once. Long cooking, a lid or acid turns green vegetables olive-drab, so add lemon or vinegar only right before serving.

### 5.7 Deglaze · Ablöschen, Deglacieren
- Triggers DE: `ablöschen`, `deglacieren`, `Bratensatz lösen`, `loskochen`. Caution: `ablöschen` is often used loosely for any liquid added to a hot pan, so it signals "liquid into a hot pan", which is enough. EN: `deglaze`, `scrape up the browned bits`, `fond`.
- Tips:
  - Scrape up the fond while the liquid boils: the browned residue is the base of the sauce's flavour. Burnt (black) residue tastes bitter and should be discarded.
  - Reduce wine well before adding cream or stock, so the raw alcohol and acid soften.

### 5.8 Confit · Confieren (Konfieren)
- Triggers DE: `confieren`, `konfieren`, `im eigenen Fett garen`, `in Öl confieren`, `Confit`. EN: `confit`, `slowly cook in fat`, `submerged in oil/fat`.
- Tips:
  - Cook submerged in fat at a low temperature (about 80 to 95 °C, far below frying) for hours; traditional duck confit is salt-cured first, which seasons and firms the meat (**CIA**).
  - Garlic or herbs stored in oil at room temperature can allow botulism; keep them refrigerated and use them within a few days (**NCHFP** advises about 4 days) **[uncertain on exact days: verify on NCHFP]**.

### 5.9 Caramelize · Karamellisieren
- Triggers DE: `karamellisieren`, `Karamell`, `goldbraun`, `Zucker schmelzen`, `glasieren` (partly). EN: `caramelize`, `caramel`, `until amber`, `dry caramel`, `wet caramel`.
- Tips:
  - Sugar itself caramelizes at high temperatures (sucrose from roughly 160 °C) (**McGEE**). "Caramelized onions" are mostly Maillard browning plus sugar caramelization and take **30 to 45 minutes or more** on low to medium heat, not the 10 minutes many recipes claim.
  - For a wet caramel, don't stir once the sugar dissolves and wash sugar crystals off the pan sides: a stray crystal can make the whole syrup recrystallize. A little glucose syrup or lemon juice prevents it.

### 5.10 Maillard reaction · Maillard-Reaktion (Röstaromen)
- Triggers DE: `rösten`, `anrösten`, `bräunen`, `Röstaromen`, `goldbraun braten`, `scharf anbraten`, `Tomatenmark anrösten`, `im Ofen rösten`. EN: `brown`, `roast`, `toast`, `golden brown`, `until browned`.
- Tips:
  - It needs a dry surface and temperatures well above boiling (noticeably from about 140 °C) (**McGEE**). Wet food stays at 100 °C until the water is gone, so it can't brown.
  - A slightly alkaline surface speeds it up: a pinch of baking soda browns onions faster, at some cost in texture (**McGEE**). Too much soda tastes soapy.

## 6. Baking basics

Sources: **McGEE**, **CIA**, standard German packaging guidance (Dr. Oetker conventions) where noted.

- **Yeast · Hefe**
  - Fresh vs dry: one cube of fresh yeast (42 g) ≈ 2 sachets dry yeast (2 × 7 g); one sachet of dry yeast is meant for about 500 g flour. So fresh : dry ≈ 3 : 1 by weight **[some sources say 2.5 : 1]**.
  - Temperature: liquid for yeast should be lukewarm (around 30 to 37 °C). Yeast slows below about 10 °C and dies from about 55 to 60 °C (**McGEE**). "Hot milk" with yeast is a common mistake.
  - **Yeast and salt:** salt slows yeast and tightens gluten; normal amounts are about 1.8 to 2 % of flour weight. Contact in a dry mix for minutes is harmless; a long soak of yeast in strong brine weakens it. The "never let them touch" rule is overstated **[uncertain: widely repeated, weak evidence]**. Keep as a low-priority info.
  - Sugar in large amounts (sweet doughs over about 10 % of flour) slows yeast; such doughs rise slower.
- **Baking soda · Natron (Speisenatron)**
  - Pure sodium bicarbonate. Needs an **acid** to release CO₂ and to avoid a soapy, metallic taste: buttermilk, yogurt, sour cream, lemon juice, vinegar, natural (non-dutched) cocoa, honey, molasses, brown sugar.
  - Reacts as soon as it's wet, so the batter should go into the oven promptly.
  - Too much: soapy taste, yellowish or greenish crumb, coarse texture.
- **Baking powder · Backpulver**
  - Baking soda plus a dry acid and starch. Most of the gas comes when wet and heated; doesn't need an acidic ingredient. German retail: one sachet (≈ 15 to 16 g) for 500 g flour **[uncertain: brand-dependent]**; US: about 1 to 1¼ teaspoons per 125 g flour.
  - Too much: rises fast then collapses, bitter or salty taste, coarse crumb.
  - Old baking powder loses strength. Test: a spoon in hot water should fizz.
- **Creaming · Schaumig rühren**
  - Beating soft butter with sugar traps air bubbles that baking powder gas later enlarges.
  - Butter should be soft but cool (around 18 to 21 °C, bending without melting). Melted or very warm butter can't hold air; cold butter doesn't cream.
  - Eggs at room temperature, added one at a time; cold eggs or adding them all at once can make the batter curdle (split). A spoon of flour rescues it.
- **Folding · Unterheben**
  - Gently turning a light mixture (whipped egg whites, cream, sponge foam) into a heavier one with a spatula, to keep the air. Stirring or beating knocks it out and gives a flat, dense result.
  - Lighten first: stir a third of the foam in, then fold in the rest.
- **Blind baking · Blindbacken**
  - Pre-baking a pastry case lined with paper and weights (dried beans, rice, ceramic beads; Hülsenfrüchte) before a wet filling goes in. Prevents a soggy bottom and stops the sides slumping.
  - Prick the base (stippen), chill the lined tin before baking (less shrinking).
- **Resting dough · Teig ruhen lassen**
  - Shortcrust and pastry: chill (kalt stellen) so gluten relaxes and butter firms; prevents shrinking and toughness.
  - Pasta and bread: rest lets flour hydrate and gluten relax, so the dough rolls out easily.
  - Crêpe and pancake batter: resting (about 30 min) hydrates flour and lets bubbles escape, for a more tender result.
- **Typical mistakes and causes**
  - Tough, tunnelled muffins: overmixing after flour is added (gluten develops). Mix only until just combined.
  - Collapsed cake: oven door opened too early, underbaked, too much raising agent or sugar, oven too cool.
  - Domed and cracked cake: oven too hot.
  - Dense bread: yeast killed by hot liquid, too little rise time, cold room, old yeast, too much salt.
  - Overproofed bread: rises then collapses, sour taste, large holes.
  - Soggy pastry base: no blind baking, too wet a filling, oven too cool, bottom shelf not used.
  - Shrunk pastry: not rested or chilled, dough stretched into the tin.
  - Dry baked goods: flour measured by volume and packed (cups vary by 20 % or more); overbaked. Weighing is more reliable.
  - Sunken fruit: batter too thin; tossing fruit in flour helps a little.
  - Brownies or cookies too cakey: too much egg or flour, too much creaming.

## 7. Food safety

Official sources: **USDA FSIS** (US), **FSA** (UK), **BfR** (Germany), **FSAI** (Ireland), **CDC/FDA** (US), **EU-853** (EU). Where agencies differ, both are given. Wokko should state which source it uses; I suggest citing BfR/FSA (70 °C for 2 min) as primary for a German/European audience and USDA as the second figure.

### 7.1 Core temperatures

| Food | EU / UK / DE | USDA FSIS |
|---|---|---|
| Poultry (all, incl. ground and stuffing) | ≥ 70 °C in the core for **2 min**, meat uniformly white (**BfR-POULTRY**, **FSA-COOK**) | **74 °C (165 °F)** |
| Minced meat (beef, pork, lamb), sausages, burgers | 70 °C for 2 min, no pink, juices clear (**FSA-COOK**); BfR: cook through. LGL Bayern gives 70 °C for **10 min** **[uncertain: conflicts with BfR's 2 min]** | **71 °C (160 °F)** |
| Pork, whole cuts | Germany/UK traditionally "cook through" (70 °C); UK allows whole cuts of beef/lamb pink, not pork **[uncertain on current German pork wording]** | **63 °C (145 °F) + 3 min rest** |
| Beef, lamb, veal whole cuts (steak, roast) | Whole cuts may be served pink; the bacteria are on the surface, which searing kills (**FSA-COOK**) | 63 °C (145 °F) + 3 min rest |
| Fish | Cook until opaque and flakes (**FSA-COOK**) | **63 °C (145 °F)** |
| Fish to be eaten raw or lightly cooked (sushi, ceviche, gravlax) | Must be frozen first: **−20 °C for ≥ 24 h** or **−35 °C for ≥ 15 h** against parasites (**EU-853**), for wild fish especially | FDA has similar parasite-freezing guidance |
| Reheating leftovers | Steaming hot throughout, **70 °C for 2 min** (**FSA-COOK**, **FSAI-SV**); reheat only once (**FSS-RICE** for rice) | **74 °C (165 °F)** |
| Egg dishes (casseroles, quiche) | Cook until set | 71 °C (160 °F) |

FSA time-temperature equivalents to 70 °C for 2 min: 60 °C for 45 min, 65 °C for 10 min, 75 °C for 30 s, 80 °C for 6 s (**FSA-COOK** business guidance). Useful for sous vide rules.

### 7.2 Raw egg dishes · Speisen mit rohen Eiern
- Mayonnaise, aioli, tiramisu, mousse au chocolat, raw cake creams, eggnog, Caesar dressing, steak tartare with yolk, some ice cream.
- **BfR**: dishes that aren't heated before eating should preferably be made without raw egg; raw eggs should be kept at ≤ 7 °C and used quickly (**BfR-EGG**). Children under five, pregnant people, older people and people with weakened immunity should avoid them.
- **LGL**: eat right away, or chill rapidly to below 7 °C and eat within 24 hours (**LGL**).
- Fix: pasteurized eggs, or heat the yolks (e.g. sabayon method over a water bath).
- Salmonella can survive and grow at fridge temperature slowly; freezing doesn't kill it.

### 7.3 Rice left out · Gekochter Reis
- *Bacillus cereus* spores survive cooking; at room temperature they germinate and make a heat-stable toxin, so reheating doesn't make it safe (**FSS-RICE**, **FDA-BBB**).
- Cool cooked rice quickly (ideally within 1 hour), refrigerate, eat within 24 hours, reheat only once until steaming hot (**FSS-RICE**).
- Applies to fried rice from yesterday's rice, rice salads, sushi rice left out for hours, also pasta (less often cited).

### 7.4 Cooling stock and large pots · Brühe und große Mengen abkühlen
- Refrigerate cooked food within **2 hours** (1 hour above 32 °C/90 °F) (**USDA-LEFT**).
- A big pot of stock or soup cools too slowly in one piece, even in the fridge. Divide into shallow containers, or cool in an ice/cold water bath, stirring (**USDA-LEFT**).
- Keep in the fridge 3 to 4 days, freeze for longer (**USDA-LEFT**).
- Don't leave it on the stove overnight to cool.

### 7.5 Raw flour and dough · Rohes Mehl und roher Teig
- Flour is a raw agricultural product, not treated against germs; outbreaks of *E. coli* (STEC) and *Salmonella* have been traced to it (**CDC-FLOUR**).
- Don't eat raw dough or batter, including no-bake recipes made with untreated flour; baking or boiling kills the bacteria. Heat-treated flour exists commercially; home heat-treatment is not reliably validated **[uncertain]**.

### 7.6 Additional, detectable hazards
- **Dried red kidney beans · Rote Kidneybohnen (getrocknet)**: contain phytohaemagglutinin (a lectin). Soak, then **boil hard for at least 10 minutes**; slow cookers at low settings can make it worse than raw (**FDA-BBB**). Canned beans are already cooked.
- **Don't wash raw poultry**: splashing spreads Campylobacter (**BfR-POULTRY**, FSA).
- **Hackfleisch / Mett**: raw minced pork (Mett) is a traditional German dish; BfR advises risk groups to avoid raw meat **[source: BfR general advice; verify a direct page if used]**.

## 8. Problem rules

Format: **Condition** (over classes, amounts, units, step order, words) → **Cause** → **Fix**. Severity: W = warning, I = info. Sources as listed.

1. **Acid into dairy over heat** · W
   Condition: a step adds `acid.*` (wine, lemon, vinegar, tomato) to `liquid.milk`, `dairy.cream` (< 30 % fat), `dairy.yogurt` or `dairy.sour_cream` while heating (`kochen`, `köcheln`, `simmer`, `boil`), and no starch is in the sauce.
   Cause: acid and heat make milk proteins clump (curdling). Low-fat dairy is most sensitive; heavy cream and crème fraîche tolerate it (**McGEE**).
   Fix: reduce the acid first, then add cream; use cream ≥ 30 % or crème fraîche; or bind with a little starch.

2. **Cornstarch or potato starch slurry, then long cooking** · W
   Condition: `starch.corn` or `starch.potato` added in a step followed by ≥ 10 min of boiling/simmering, or added at the start of a long braise.
   Cause: long boiling and stirring break the swollen starch granules; the sauce thins (**McGEE**, **ATK**).
   Fix: add the slurry at the end and boil it only briefly.

3. **Salt before a long reduction** · I
   Condition: salt (or salty stock cubes, soy sauce) added in a step before `reduzieren`/`einkochen`/`reduce by half` or a simmer ≥ 60 min.
   Cause: evaporation concentrates the salt.
   Fix: season at the end (plan's first batch).

4. **Hot roux plus hot liquid** · I
   Condition: roux step, then a step adding stock/milk described as `heiß`/`hot`/`kochend`, all at once (`auf einmal`, `all at once`).
   Cause: starch on the outside gels before the inside is wet; lumps.
   Fix: cold or room-temperature liquid into the hot roux, added gradually, whisking.

5. **Roux sauce never boiled** · I
   Condition: roux + liquid, and no later step with `aufkochen`/`kochen`/`simmer`/`boil`.
   Cause: starch doesn't fully swell, so the sauce tastes floury and can turn pasty.
   Fix: bring to a boil and simmer at least a few minutes.

6. **Egg yolk liaison boiled** · W (I if starch present)
   Condition: `egg.yolk` added to a hot sauce, then `kochen`/`aufkochen`/`boil`, and no `starch.*`.
   Cause: yolk proteins curdle somewhere around 70 to 85 °C (**McGEE**).
   Fix: temper the yolks and keep the sauce below a simmer; with flour already in the sauce it is safe.

7. **Gelatine with raw protease fruit** · W
   Condition: `gelling.gelatine` with raw `fruit.pineapple`, `fruit.kiwi`, `fruit.papaya`, `fruit.fig` (fresh), `fruit.mango`, and no cooking step for the fruit, and not canned.
   Cause: protein-digesting enzymes stop the gel.
   Fix: use canned or cooked fruit, or agar.

8. **Gelatine boiled** · I
   Condition: gelatine in a step with `kochen`/`aufkochen`/`boil`.
   Cause: prolonged heat weakens the gel.
   Fix: dissolve in warm liquid off the boil.

9. **Agar not boiled** · W
   Condition: `gelling.agar` and no boil step after adding it.
   Cause: agar only dissolves at a boil (**FAO-AGAR**).
   Fix: boil 1 to 2 min.

10. **Baking soda without acid** · W
    Condition: `leavening.baking_soda` and none of: buttermilk, yogurt, sour cream, lemon, vinegar, natural cocoa, honey, molasses, brown sugar, (also baking powder present cancels the warning).
    Cause: no CO₂ release, soapy taste.
    Fix: add an acidic ingredient or use baking powder.

11. **Yeast into hot liquid** · W
    Condition: `leavening.yeast` and a step with the liquid `heiß`/`kochend`/`hot`/`boiling`, or a stated temperature ≥ 50 °C.
    Cause: yeast dies from about 55 to 60 °C.
    Fix: lukewarm liquid (about 30 to 37 °C).

12. **Raw egg in an uncooked dish** · I (safety)
    Condition: `egg.*` and no step that heats it (no bake/cook/boil/Wasserbad after the egg is added), e.g. mayonnaise, tiramisu, mousse.
    Cause: Salmonella risk (**BfR-EGG**).
    Fix: pasteurized eggs or heated yolks; chill ≤ 7 °C, eat soon; not for risk groups.

13. **Poultry or minced meat with no cooking step** · W (safety)
    Condition: `meat.poultry` or `meat.minced` and no heating step for it (plan already lists this).
    Cause: Salmonella, Campylobacter, STEC.
    Fix: cook to 70 °C core for 2 min (**BfR-POULTRY**, **FSA-COOK**).

14. **Dried kidney beans not boiled hard** · W (safety)
    Condition: `legume.kidney_bean` (dried, not canned) and either no soaking + boil step, or only slow cooker/`Slow Cooker`/low heat.
    Cause: lectin toxin destroyed only by a hard boil (**FDA-BBB**).
    Fix: soak, then boil hard ≥ 10 min before slow cooking.

15. **Raw flour in a no-bake recipe** · I (safety)
    Condition: `starch.wheat_flour` and no bake/cook step (no-bake cookie dough, cake truffles).
    Cause: raw flour can carry E. coli/Salmonella (**CDC-FLOUR**).
    Fix: use heat-treated flour.

16. **Cooked rice kept for later** · I (safety)
    Condition: `grain.rice` cooked, and a step says `über Nacht`, `vom Vortag`, `day-old`, `leftover`, `abkühlen lassen` without fridge cue.
    Cause: Bacillus cereus toxin survives reheating (**FSS-RICE**).
    Fix: cool within an hour, fridge, eat within 24 h.

17. **Large batch cooling on the stove** · I (safety)
    Condition: stock/soup with ≥ 3 l total liquid and `abkühlen lassen`/`let cool`/`über Nacht stehen lassen` without fridge or ice bath.
    Cause: big volumes cool too slowly (**USDA-LEFT**).
    Fix: split into shallow containers, or use a cold water bath; fridge within 2 h.

18. **Overcrowded pan when searing** · I [heuristic]
    Condition: ≥ 800 g meat (or diced meat for stew) in one `anbraten`/`sear` step without `portionsweise`/`in batches`.
    Cause: temperature drops, meat steams grey instead of browning.
    Fix: sear in batches.

19. **Wet meat seared** · I [heuristic]
    Condition: a marinade or brine step directly followed by a sear step, without `trocken tupfen`/`pat dry`.
    Cause: water at the surface stops browning until it has boiled off.
    Fix: pat dry first.

20. **Garlic burnt in a hard sear** · I [heuristic]
    Condition: `allium.garlic` added in the same step as `scharf anbraten`/`sear`, before meat or onions have cooked several minutes.
    Cause: small garlic pieces burn within a minute or two at searing heat and turn bitter.
    Fix: add garlic later, for the last minute before the liquid.

21. **Delicate herbs or zest cooked from the start** · I
    Condition: `herb.delicate` (basil, parsley leaves, coriander leaves, chives, dill, chervil, tarragon) or `citrus.zest` in the first step of a cook ≥ 30 min.
    Cause: aromatic oils evaporate or degrade (plan first batch).
    Fix: add at the end; stems and woody herbs (bay, thyme, rosemary) can go in early.

22. **Chicken breast simmered or braised for long** · I
    Condition: `meat.chicken.breast` with braise/simmer ≥ 45 min.
    Cause: lean muscle without collagen dries out (plan first batch).
    Fix: use thighs or a soup hen, or add the breast late.

23. **Butter emulsion overheated** · W
    Condition: hollandaise/béarnaise/beurre blanc pattern (yolk or reduction + butter) with `kochen`/`aufkochen`/`boil`/`high heat` after the butter is in.
    Cause: emulsion breaks; yolks scramble (**McGEE**).
    Fix: water bath or low heat; rescue a split sauce by whisking it into a spoon of warm water or a new yolk.

24. **Mayonnaise oil added all at once** · I
    Condition: mayonnaise pattern, oil in the same step with `auf einmal`/`all at once`, no `tropfenweise`/`drop by drop`/`in a thin stream`, and no stick blender.
    Cause: the yolk can't emulsify that much oil at once.
    Fix: add oil drop by drop at first, then in a thin stream; ingredients at the same temperature. (A stick blender in a tall jar works with all the oil at once.)

25. **Green vegetables with acid or long covered cooking** · I
    Condition: `veg.green` (beans, broccoli, peas, spinach) with acid in the same cooking step, or cooked covered ≥ 10 min.
    Cause: acid and long heat turn chlorophyll olive-drab (**McGEE**).
    Fix: cook briefly, uncovered, in plenty of water; add acid at serving.

26. **Dried beans cooked with acid from the start** · I
    Condition: dried `legume.*` with tomatoes, vinegar, wine or molasses in the first cooking step.
    Cause: acid (and calcium) keep the cell walls firm; the beans stay hard (**McGEE**). Salt does **not** have this effect; salting early is fine.
    Fix: cook the beans soft first, then add the acid.

27. **Yogurt or low-fat dairy boiled in a curry or stew** · I
    Condition: `dairy.yogurt`, `dairy.quark`, `dairy.sour_cream`, or `liquid.milk` with `kochen`/`boil` after adding it, no starch.
    Cause: proteins curdle when boiled.
    Fix: stir in off the heat, temper, or stabilize with a little starch or flour.

28. **Cheese added to a boiling sauce** · I
    Condition: `dairy.cheese` added in a step with `kochen`/`boil` or `bei starker Hitze`.
    Cause: the cheese proteins clump and the fat separates (stringy, greasy).
    Fix: take the sauce off the heat, then stir in the cheese gradually.

29. **Wet caramel stirred** · I [heuristic]
    Condition: sugar + water `karamellisieren`/`caramelize` and `rühren`/`stir` after it boils.
    Cause: crystallization.
    Fix: swirl the pan instead; wash down crystals; a little glucose or lemon.

30. **Flour or sugar in volume units for baking** · I
    Condition: baking recipe (`bake`/`backen`, oven) with `starch.wheat_flour` or sugar in `cup`/`Tasse`/`EL` ≥ 4.
    Cause: a cup of flour varies by 20 % or more with how it's filled.
    Fix: weigh; Wokko can show the gram estimate.

Also worth carrying over from the plan: hot roux + hot liquid (rule 4), stock started in hot or salted water, light dish with roasted bones or tomato paste turning dark, missing amounts/units, ingredients no step uses.

## 9. Substitutions

Ratios are common kitchen practice; sources noted. All substitutions change allergens and diet status, so Wokko should recompute those.

1. **Buttermilk · Buttermilch**: 250 ml milk + 1 tbsp (15 ml) lemon juice or white vinegar, stand 5 to 10 min. Or yogurt thinned with milk (about 2 : 1). Works where buttermilk activates baking soda.
2. **Self-raising flour · Mehl mit Backpulver (Self-raising flour)**: about 5 g baking powder per 100 g plain flour (King Arthur: 1 US cup ≈ 120 g flour + 1½ tsp baking powder + ¼ tsp salt) (**KA**).
3. **Egg in baking, binding · Ei als Bindung**: 1 egg ≈ 1 tbsp ground flaxseed (Leinsamen, geschrotet) + 3 tbsp water, rested 5 to 10 min. Works in muffins, cookies, pancakes; not in sponge cakes, soufflés or custards, where egg is the structure.
4. **Egg in baking, moisture · Ei als Feuchtigkeit**: 1 egg ≈ 60 g (¼ cup) mashed banana or apple purée. Adds flavour and density.
5. **Egg white · Eiweiß**: aquafaba (chickpea cooking liquid): about 2 tbsp ≈ 1 white, 3 tbsp ≈ 1 whole egg. Whips to meringue; needs longer whipping, often a pinch of cream of tartar.
6. **Fresh vs dried herbs · Frische vs. getrocknete Kräuter**: about 3 : 1 (1 tbsp fresh ≈ 1 tsp dried). Dried herbs go in early, fresh late. Delicate herbs (basil, parsley, coriander, chives, chervil) dry poorly; prefer frozen.
7. **Fresh vs dry yeast · Frischhefe vs. Trockenhefe**: 42 g fresh (1 cube) ≈ 14 g dry (2 sachets); about 3 : 1 by weight **[some sources 2.5 : 1]**. Instant yeast can be mixed into flour directly.
8. **Baking powder from baking soda · Backpulver aus Natron**: 1 tsp baking powder ≈ ¼ tsp baking soda + ½ tsp cream of tartar (Weinstein). Use at once.
9. **Cornstarch vs flour as thickener · Stärke vs. Mehl**: 1 part cornstarch ≈ 2 parts flour by weight (**ATK**). Cornstarch gives a glossy, clear result; flour an opaque one.
10. **Potato starch vs cornstarch · Kartoffelstärke vs. Maisstärke**: about 1 : 1; potato starch thickens at lower temperature and thins sooner with boiling, so add it at the end (**McGEE**).
11. **Gelatine sheets vs powder · Blatt- vs. gemahlene Gelatine**: German retail: 6 sheets ≈ 1 sachet ground (≈ 9 g), sets about 500 ml **[uncertain: check the brand; bloom strength varies]**.
12. **Gelatine → agar · Gelatine → Agar-Agar**: no fixed conversion; start at about 0.8 to 1 g agar per 100 ml for a firm gel, boil it, and test **[uncertain]**. Texture is firmer and more brittle.
13. **Sour cream vs Greek yogurt vs crème fraîche · Saure Sahne / Schmand / Joghurt / Crème fraîche**: 1 : 1 in cold uses. For hot sauces use crème fraîche or cream (≥ 30 % fat); yogurt and sour cream curdle when boiled.
14. **Cream for cooking (not whipping) · Kochsahne-Ersatz**: about 3 parts milk + 1 part melted butter by volume for 1 part cream **[heuristic]**. Won't whip; less stable in acidic sauces.
15. **Wine in cooking · Wein beim Kochen**: replace with the same amount of stock plus a splash of vinegar or lemon juice (about 1 tbsp per 250 ml), or verjus, or unsweetened grape juice with vinegar.
16. **Brown sugar · Brauner Zucker**: 200 g white sugar + 1 tbsp (≈ 20 g) molasses ≈ light brown sugar; 2 tbsp for dark.
17. **Butter vs oil in baking · Butter vs. Öl**: butter is about 80 to 82 % fat, so use about 80 % of the butter weight as oil. Not for creamed batters or laminated doughs, where solid fat is the structure.
18. **Cake flour · Kuchenmehl (US cake flour)**: 1 US cup all-purpose minus 2 tbsp, plus 2 tbsp cornstarch. German type 405 already sits close to US pastry/all-purpose flour **[uncertain: flour types don't map exactly; 405 ≈ pastry/all-purpose, 550 ≈ all-purpose/bread]**.
19. **Fish sauce (vegetarian/vegan) · Fischsauce-Ersatz**: soy sauce plus a little seaweed (kombu, nori) or miso; changes allergens to soy and often wheat.
20. **Worcestershire sauce · Worcestershiresauce-Ersatz**: soy sauce + a little vinegar + a pinch of sugar, or a vegan Worcestershire **[heuristic]**. Changes allergens (soy, wheat).

## Open questions and doubts

- **Liaison/hollandaise curdling temperature:** sources range from about 70 to 85 °C. Rules should avoid a precise number and say "below a simmer".
- **Minced meat time at 70 °C:** BfR uses 2 min; LGL Bayern says 10 min. Use BfR.
- **Pork whole cuts:** USDA allows 63 °C + rest; German consumer advice usually says "cook through". Rule text should show both or pick one source explicitly.
- **Potato starch gelation temperature and boiling tolerance:** only approximate figures found.
- **Agar strength and gelatine/sheet equivalents:** brand-dependent. Ratios are starting points.
- **Grana Padano lysozyme (egg):** not confirmed against the consortium's specification.
- **Worcestershire sauce gluten:** depends on the vinegar used by the brand and market.
- **Yeast-salt contact:** commonly repeated, weak evidence; keep it low priority as the plan already says.
- **Garlic-in-oil storage days:** check NCHFP's exact advice before shipping.
- **EUR-Lex, USDA and foodsafety.gov pages** blocked automated fetching during research; their figures above come from my knowledge of the texts plus search-result confirmations (FSIS figures matched in search snippets from FSIS and state health departments). A person should open Annex II once to confirm the exemption wording.
- **Beurre blanc holding temperature:** varying advice; give no hard number.
